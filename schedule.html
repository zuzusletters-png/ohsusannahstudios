Zuzu’s Letters · monthly working schedule

The Monthly Envelope

Working backwards from the postbox. Every date below is driven by one fixed point — the letter has to be in her hands before the month ends.

The two fixed dates

The 15th — orders close for this month’s letter. After this you know your exact count.

The 26th — everything must be in the post. Anything later risks missing the month.

That leaves eleven days between knowing your number and needing to be finished. Everything creative has to happen before the 15th, not after.

1st – 7thWrite

The part only you can do

  • Draft this month’s letter
  • Choose the photograph that becomes the print
  • Start gathering songs for the playlist

The letter is the one thing that cannot be rushed, bought, or delegated. Give it the freshest part of the month. Everything after this is production.

8th – 10thOrder prints

Get the long-lead item moving

  • Send the image to be printed
  • Order last month’s count plus about a quarter more
  • Choose the quote for the bookmark card

Printing is the only step that depends on somebody else’s schedule, so it goes first. Ordering before you know your final number feels wrong — but spare prints are not waste. They are next month’s spares, replacements for damaged post, and stock you can sell on their own.

11th – 14thFinish making

Everything that isn’t the envelope

  • Final letter text, proofread
  • Finish the playlist and make the QR code
  • Design the quote card

Proofread on paper, not on screen. You will catch things you have read past a dozen times.

15thOrders close

The hinge of the month

  • Pull the mailing list — ask Claude, or export from Stripe
  • Check it against refunds and cancellations
  • Count. If you are short on prints, top up today

Everything before this is creative and flexible. Everything after is production with a deadline. This is the day the month changes character.

16th – 20thPrint & prepare

Make the pieces

  • Print the letters in the script font
  • Print the quote cards and the playlist QR inserts
  • Stamp the postcards that go on to a friend
  • Address the outer envelopes

Address the envelopes before you stuff them. It is far easier to bin a mis-addressed empty envelope than to unpack a full one.

21st – 25thAssemble

Fill the envelopes

  • Letter, print, quote card, two postcards, the surprises
  • Stamp the outer envelopes
  • Tick each name off the list as you seal it

Lay out one complete envelope first and check it against the list of what is promised on the website. Then use it as the model for the rest. Assembling from memory is how one person ends up without their print.

26thPost

Everything goes in the post

  • Take the lot to the post office
  • Mark the mailing list as posted

First class runs three to five working days. Posting on the 26th gets a letter to most of the country before the month turns. Posting on the 28th does not, and you have promised the month.

27th – 31stTidy up

Close the month

  • Add new subscribers to the Facebook group
  • File the mailing list as your record of what went out
  • Note the count — it sets next month’s print order
  • Jot down anything you want to write about next time

December runs differently

Orders stay open until the 20th, which removes five days from an already tight month — in the one month the post is slowest and you are busiest.

  • Do December’s creative work in November. Letter written, image chosen, playlist finished before the 1st.
  • Order the prints in late November, not December.
  • Keep the 20th to the 23rd clear for assembly.
  • Post on the 23rd, not the 26th.

The website already says Christmas delivery is not guaranteed, so you are covered. But most of these will be gifts, and arriving in time is the whole point.

Buy these ahead, not monthly

Where these dates came from. I have assumed first class post takes three to five working days, and that prints come back from a supplier within about a week. If you print at home, days 8–10 collapse and you gain a week of slack. If your printer is slower than a week, move the print order earlier — it is the one deadline with no give in it.

The dates are a frame, not a rule. The only two that genuinely matter are the 15th, because you promised it, and the 26th, because the post is not negotiable.
